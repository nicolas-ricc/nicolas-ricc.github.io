# mapa: domain glossary

The site is an isometric night map in three zones (Portfolio, Resume, Blog). The code, the lab and the docs share the vocabulary below; the Spanish name, where the old specs and plans use one, is in parentheses, and each entry names the file and identifier that anchor it. Exhibit ids (`#faro`, `#estacion`) and the keys of `content/*.json` stay in Spanish because they are URLs and data, not names in the code. Architecture decisions are in `docs/adr/`.

### Veil
(Velo.) The sky-colored overlay drawn over the zones that are not in focus, together with the dimming of their lights. With a focus zone (`focusAlphas(focus)`), the other two zones get a veil alpha of `VEIL_ALPHA` (0.45) and an accent alpha of `ACCENT_DIM` (0.35); the focused zone, or every zone when nothing is focused, gets veil 0 and accents 1. The veil polygons (`veilPolygons`, built from `veilCells` and `veilRuns`) cover content plus bleed, one Graphics per zone. A cell where the zones disagree is split into 6 u cells, each classified by its centre, so the veil follows a seam only to 6 u: the cell x 342..348 next to the lighthouse headland's base goes with Blog and takes the last 2 u of the promenade parapet with it. A light is veiled with the zone where it stands, except lights mounted on a ship, which follow the ship's zone: see `docs/adr/0001-veil-lights-follow-their-position.md`.
Code: `src/world/veil.ts` (`VEIL_ALPHA`, `ACCENT_DIM`, `focusAlphas`, `veilPolygons`, `accentsByZone`), `src/world/stage.ts` (`setFocus`).

### Zone
(Zona.) One of the three clickable regions of the world, `"portfolio"`, `"cv"` (Resume) or `"blog"`; the ids are also the routes. A point belongs to a zone by geography, not by a straight split: `worldZoneAt(x, y)` returns Blog for the headland, its reef and its shore east of `ZONE_SPLIT_X` (the headland moved to the island's east coast in #426, at x 344..414, y 269..306, and the `x >= ZONE_SPLIT_X` guard keeps the promenade in Resume), Resume for the seawall shore, and Blog for the open sea. The site's hit test tries the signs first (`zoneAtScreen`) and falls back to `worldZoneAt`. The main split is `ZONE_SPLIT_X` (344) plus `shoreWidth(y)`: land west of it is Portfolio for y < `ZONE_SPLIT_Y` (90) and Resume for y >= 90, except `STATION_LAND` (the terminal station, x 96..198, y 90..146), which stays Portfolio; east of it is Blog. The same function classifies the veil cells, the static lights and the hit test.
Code: `src/map/geo.ts` (`WorldZone`, `worldZoneAt`, `ZONE_SPLIT_Y`, `STATION_LAND`, `HEADLAND`, `HEADLAND_SHIFT`), `src/world/veil.ts` (`WORLD_ZONES`), `src/world/signs.ts` (`zoneAtScreen`).

### Rail lands
(Playas ferroviarias.) The band `y 129..158` of Resume around the west train track, just south of the seam and the station land: freight lots, a siding with a parked freight train and a fence on its edges. The fence and everything south of the track obey the train's occlusion rule of the station (south of the track, `y_min − 1.4·z_max ≥ 134.5`), so that nothing hides the stopped or moving train.
Code: `src/city/rail-lands.ts`, `src/scenes/station.ts` (the train rule, `TRAIN_CLEAR`).

### Accent
(Acento.) An artificial light, drawn on top of everything with additive blend: a `dot` (lamp: position `at`, radius `r`, color) or a `poly` (lit window, beam: points, color, optional alpha). The functional lights use `spot()` (`spot(at, r, color, alpha)`), which returns a haloless diamond `poly` (lighthouse gallery, train, signals, buffer stops, wreck buoy, marquee bulbs). Accents are not solids and cast no shadow; the bloom (or its `fallback` halo) is drawn around them. Each accent belongs to a zone for the veil (`accentZone`: the point of a dot or the first vertex of a polygon, through `worldZoneAt`).
Code: `src/iso/accent.ts` (`Accent`, `accentItem`, `spot`), `src/world/veil.ts` (`accentZone`), `src/world/frame.ts` (`drawAccents`).

### Animator
(Animador.) The Pixi-free contract between an animated scene and the runtime (`Animator`): the `ids` of its layers, `layer(id)` (an `AnimLayer`: a `solid`, `accent` or `water` layer), `tick(dtMs)` (the set of layer ids that need redrawing) and optional `claims` (terrain water it draws itself). `assembleWorld` returns `{ scene, animators, platform }`: `platform` is the `PlatformAnimator` of the Writing platform (`null` when the zones assembled leave out the sea), registered (tag "blog") right before the sea animator, so the site can call `setSite`. Every animator in `animators` is tagged with its zone of origin (`ZonedAnimator`, `tag(animator, zone)`; `null` for the water). That tag serves the lab and the crops; the veil of its lights does not use it, only the zone a layer declares in `AnimLayer.zone` (see Veil).
Code: `src/scenes/animator.ts` (`Animator`, `AnimLayer`, `layerLookup`), `src/world/assemble.ts` (`ZonedAnimator`, `tag`, `assembleWorld`).

### Hull
(Casco.) The solid that is a ship's body up to its deck, lofted with sheer at bow and stern (`kind: "hull"`: `len`, `beam`, `h`, topsides `topMat`, `sheer`, default `HULL_SHEER`). Deck pieces and side pieces are placed against it by the hull rule.
Code: `src/iso/solids.ts` (`Solid` kind `hull`, `HULL_SHEER`), `src/scenes/ships.ts` (`ShipSpec`, `kit`).

### Deck piece
(Pieza de cubierta.) A non-hull solid that stands on a ship's hull and overlaps it in plan. In the code (`hullSide`, in the sort frame, steps 1 and 2) it is one of two cases: its base is at or above the midship deck (`hull.at.z + hull.h`), or all four corners of its plan box lie inside the hull's deck ring and it rises above the deck. It is always drawn after the hull. A piece inside the deck ring that stays below the deck (sunk under it) is drawn before the hull.
Code: `hullSide` in `src/iso/depth.ts`; vocabulary from `docs/plans/2026-10-19-74-orden-piezas-barcos.md` ("Vocabulario") and `docs/plans/2026-11-02-195-lado-por-cara-del-casco.md`.

### Side piece
(Pieza de costado.) A non-hull solid that overlaps the hull in plan but is neither a deck piece nor wholly under the keel (its top is more than 1e-6 above the hull box's lowest point). It hangs from a hull face. The face is the one of the hull's box against which the piece's center is farthest out, normalized by the hull half-extent along each axis (`u` along x, `w` along y; the larger of `|u|` and `|w|` decides, ties go to x). The outward normal of that face, projected on the view in the ship's frame (`vx`, `vy`), gives the side `t`: the piece is drawn after the hull if `t > 0` (near side), before it if `t < 0` (far side), and no rule applies if `|t| <= 1e-6` (view parallel to the face) or the piece is centered.
Code: `hullSide` in `src/iso/depth.ts`.

### Hull rule
(Regla del casco.) The three rules of #74 that `sortByDepth` applies to hulls, at any heading and also in static scenes (the ships, the shipyard hulls, the fair boats). (1) The side of a non-hull piece against a hull is decided by `hullSide` (deck piece or side piece, above), not by the depth key. (2) The hull separates: if one piece goes before a hull and another goes after it, the first goes before the second (`hullOrder`; if two hulls disagree, the first in the list wins). (3) A view parallel to the plane that separates two solids (view component within 1e-6) hides neither: separating along it counts as behind in both directions (`farAlong`, `isBehindView`). #195 changed rule 1 from the centroid test to the hull face the piece hangs from.
Code: `src/iso/depth.ts` (`sortByDepth`, `hullSide`, `hullOrder`, `farAlong`); `src/scenes/ships.ts`; tests `src/iso/depth.test.ts`, `src/scenes/ships-depth.test.ts`.

### Boom
(Pluma.) The inclined arm of a deck crane, rising from the cab toward the bow and carrying the hook. Avoid "jib", which names the arm of the tower crane in `pieces.ts`.
Code: `src/scenes/ships.ts` (deck cranes of the cargo ship).

### Stay
(Estay.) The taut cable from a deck crane's cab roof to the tip of its boom.
Code: `src/scenes/ships.ts` (deck cranes of the cargo ship).

### Frame
(Marco.) A ship's local frame: the axes in which its solids are axis-aligned, x toward the bow and y to starboard, rotated by the ship's `heading`. `kit(at, heading)` in `ships.ts` builds the ship's pieces in it; `sortByDepth(solids, heading)` rotates the boxes and the view by `-heading` to evaluate depth and the hull rule in it (`vx`, `vy` are the view components in that frame); an `AnimLayer` of kind `solid` carries its `heading` for that sort. Not to be confused with `Frame` in `src/world/frame.ts` (`WorldZone | "all" | "cover"`), the camera framing of the page, which has its own functions `zoneFrame`, `coverFrame` and `boxFrame`; the storybook frames with `contentFrame` instead.
Code: `src/iso/depth.ts` (`sortByDepth`, `worldAndFrameBounds`), `src/scenes/ships.ts` (`kit`), `src/scenes/animator.ts` (`AnimLayer.heading`).

### Lanes
(Carriles.) The two nearly straight routes the ships follow between the river mouth and the southern edge of the cover: the outbound lane `LANE_OUT` starts at the mouth (362, −16), runs off the island's east coast and heads south-southeast, east of the moved headland's tip and of Writing's narrow site, until it leaves the cover; the return lane `LANE_BACK` enters from the bottom, passes east of the wreck and west of Writing's wide site, runs about 42 u east of the outbound lane on the northern stretch and ends at the mouth (397, −39). Ships move along them at `LANE_SPEED` (1.2 u/s), fade in and out (`FADE_U`, `alphaAt`) at both ends of each lane (the sea end lies in the bleed), and cycle out, off camera (`OFF_MS`), back, in port (`PORT_MS`); ship k runs at phase k·T/3 of `CYCLE_MS`. They neither anchor nor turn in place.
Code: `src/scenes/sea-anim.ts` (`LANE_OUT`, `LANE_BACK`, `LANE_SPEED`, `shipStateAt`, `CYCLE_MS`); introduced in #87, reshaped in #425 (story #423).

### Exhibit
(Exhibit.) One entry of the lab's structures storybook (`/lab/storybook.html`): a structure looked at on its own. It is a `region` (a box `Box` cropped from the real world, with an optional framing height `h`), a `context` (a crop of the world, with no `build` or `animate`, that carries the `sign` the lab audits in its scene; `contextScene` and `contextAnimators` crop the world through `cropScene` and `cropAnimators`, in `src/lab/crop.ts`) or a `piece` (a relocatable piece built at the origin by `build`, with an optional ground `pad` material and an optional `animate` animator, which the storybook tags with the exhibit's zone). The `id` is the URL hash (`#faro`) and stays Spanish; `title` and `group` (`GroupId`, listed in `GROUPS`) are English labels. `pickExhibit(hash)` resolves a hash (unknown or empty gives the first) and `stepExhibit(id, ±1)` walks the index, wrapping around. Region and context exhibits are cropped strictly to the box (`cropScene` and `contextScene` cut strips, long prisms and terrain; `buildStage(…, { clip: box })` clips the shadows) and framed with `contentFrame`.
Code: `src/lab/exhibits.ts` (`Exhibit`, `EXHIBITS`, `GROUPS`, `pickExhibit`, `stepExhibit`), `src/lab/crop.ts` (`contextScene`, `contextAnimators`), `src/lab/frame.ts` (`contentFrame`), `src/lab/storybook.ts`.

### Landmark
The one reference point per zone used to frame it: the gantry crane for Portfolio, the office tower for Resume and the lighthouse for Blog (at (410, 290) since #426), in world coordinates. Not to be confused with a landmark recipe (see Landmark recipe).
Code: `src/scenes/world.ts` (`LANDMARKS`, `WorldScene.landmarks`).

### Bleed
(Sangrado.) The ring of terrain around all the content, so the cover camera never shows sky; built margins (hinterland, tech district) live on it. It extends the content by `BLEED` (`{ x: 342, y: 378 }`) on a grid of `CELL_BLEED` (18 u), and the veil covers content plus bleed.
Code: `src/map/geo.ts` (`BLEED`, `CELL_BLEED`), `src/world/veil.ts` (`veilCells`).

### Cover frame
The largest 16:9 rectangle that fits inside the projected content plus bleed, pulled in by `COVER_INSET` so it does not expose sky at the edge. It is the camera of the site (`coverView(w, h)`: at 16:9 or wider, the same rectangle with the real aspect of the host; narrower, the 16:9 one fills the host's height and the host shows a window of it centered on `COVER_FOCUS_U`, 0.39, the column of signs, #428) and of lab key `4` (`coverFrame(16/9)`). `zoneView` is clamped to it on each axis it covers (`clampToCover`).
Code: `src/map/geo.ts` (`coverQuad`, `inCoverQuad`, `COVER_INSET`), `src/world/frame.ts` (`coverFrame`), `src/world/view.ts` (`coverView`, `COVER_FOCUS_U`, `clampToCover`).

### Pan
(Arrastre.) The visitor moving the map view by dragging (mouse, touch or pen), with the arrow keys, or `0`/`Home` to recenter; map only, never in a zone. The Main view is `coverView(w, h)`, the one the map opens with and the one the recenter button («Centrar vista») tweens back to. The view is Moved when it differs from the Main view (`sameView`); only then the button shows. A pan always stays inside the bleed, and a move of at least `DRAG_THRESHOLD` px is a drag, which does not navigate, even when it starts on a sign link.
Code: `src/world/view.ts` (`panView`, `sameView`, `clampToBleed`), `src/pan.ts` (`attachPan`, `keyPan`, `DRAG_THRESHOLD`, `KEY_STEP`), `src/main.ts`.

### Plinth
(Zócalo.) The `PLINTH_H` (0.3) paving prism under a city block. A recipe rests on it unless its piece brings `ground` (painted before every solid, the plinth would hide it): `seat` runs the recipe once at street level and lifts the piece `PLINTH_H` when it has no ground.
Code: `src/kit/piece.ts` (`seat`, `liftPiece`), `src/scenes/city-pieces.ts` (`PLINTH_H`), `src/scenes/city.ts`, `src/lab/kit-scene.ts`.

### Lot
(Lote.) The rectangle a recipe draws inside: `x y w d`, the surface height `z` it rests on, the `open` sides (`Side`: `n`, `s`, `e`, `w`; avenue, water or plaza frontage) and `maxH`, the height cap above `z`. The zoning hands out one per block (`Placed.lot`); `waterTowerLot` and `FOOTBRIDGE_LOT` are their own lots. The lab uses 24 x 18 lots (`KIT_LOT`), or the `LAB_LOTS` size for the recipes that need one.
Code: `src/kit/types.ts` (`Lot`, `Side`), `src/city/zoning.ts` (`Placed`, `waterTowerLot`, `FOOTBRIDGE_LOT`), `src/lab/kit-scene.ts` (`KIT_LOT`).

### Piece
(Pieza.) What a recipe returns: `solids`, `ground` (painted before every solid), `accents`, plus the `walls`, `windows` and `slots` it publishes for the lights, an optional `courtyard` and an optional `landmarkTop`. `emptyPiece` starts one; `addBuilding` adds a solid and publishes its walls and windows; `liftPiece` raises it; `seat` sets it on its lot (see Plinth). `src/city/lights.ts` reads `windows`, `slots` and `walls`. Not to be confused with a deck piece or side piece, which are solids placed against a ship's hull, nor with an Exhibit of kind `piece`, a structure the storybook builds at the origin.
Code: `src/kit/types.ts` (`Piece`), `src/kit/piece.ts` (`emptyPiece`, `addBuilding`, `liftPiece`, `seat`), `src/city/lights.ts`.

### Recipe
(Receta.) `Recipe = (lot, rng, style) => Piece`: a function that draws a piece relative to `lot.z`. `RecipeDef` registers one: `name`, `recipe`, `family` (`modern`, `courtyard`, `composite`, `curved`, `lowrise`, `landmarks`) and the optional `landmark` flag. `RECIPES` concatenates the six families; `recipe(name)` looks one up and throws on an unknown name. A common recipe stays under `recipeCap(lot)` = `min(lot.maxH, RECIPE_MAX_H)` (18). The geometric helpers the recipes use (`prism`, `tiles`, `PLINTH_H`) come from `src/scenes/city-pieces.ts`. `src/scenes/city.ts` seats `recipe(p.recipe)` per placed block.
Code: `src/kit/types.ts` (`Recipe`, `RecipeDef`), `src/kit/index.ts` (`RECIPES`, `recipe`, `LAB_LOTS`), `src/kit/piece.ts` (`RECIPE_MAX_H`, `recipeCap`), `src/scenes/city.ts`.

### Interlock
(Interlock.) The optional `Solid.interlocks` field (`true`): metadata for the kit test only. `sortByDepth` sorts by box, so the kit contract checks that no two solids of a recipe interpenetrate by more than 1e-6 on all three axes. A pair is excused only when both solids carry the mark. All set through `interlock`: cornice and hipRoof; the tree props and the rooftopGarden cones; fence; classic pilasters; coveredMarket's thin walls; constructionSite slabs, columns, core and crane joints; the quay crane's upper joints; the vegetation, slabs and ruin block of eatenRuin; the beams and jungle of derelictHall. A body with a facade is never marked, so roof × body and cornice × body still fail. Rendering ignores the field.
Code: `src/iso/solids.ts` (`Solid.interlocks`), `src/kit/piece.ts` (`interlock`), `src/kit/kit.test.ts` (`interpenetrations`).

### Landmark recipe
(Receta landmark.) Not the Landmark of a zone, above: a `RecipeDef` with `landmark: true`. They are the seven of `src/kit/forms/landmarks.ts` (`LANDMARKS`) and three of `modern.ts` (`glassTower`, `classic`, `constructionSite`). Its cap is `LANDMARK_MAX_H` (24) instead of 18; `fitToCap` flattens a piece in z, relative to `lot.z`, so it fits `min(lot.maxH, LANDMARK_MAX_H)`. It publishes `Piece.landmarkTop`, the point it gives as its top (the highest point of the piece for the `landmarks` family, which `landmarks.test.ts` asserts; `glassTower` gives its roof, below the beacon), which `fitToCap` and `liftPiece` carry along; no scene code reads `landmarkTop` or the `landmark` flag today; only tests do (`kit.test.ts` uses the flag to choose the cap, and the family tests assert it). `src/city/zoning.ts` gives the fixed blocks and the `district` neighborhood a height ceiling of `LANDMARK_MAX_H` (the lot's `maxH` is the lower of that and the block's own cap), and sets `maxH` to `LANDMARK_MAX_H` in `waterTowerLot` and `FOOTBRIDGE_LOT`.
Code: `src/kit/piece.ts` (`LANDMARK_MAX_H`, `fitToCap`), `src/kit/types.ts` (`RecipeDef.landmark`, `Piece.landmarkTop`), `src/kit/forms/landmarks.ts` (`LANDMARKS`), `src/kit/forms/modern.ts`, `src/city/zoning.ts`.

### Style
(Estilo.) The look a recipe receives: `id` (`StyleId`: `downtown`, `bars`, `residential`, `civic`, `riverside`, `district`), `mat`, `trim`, `roof`, `tone`, `floorH` and `window` (`windowWarm` or `windowCool`). `STYLES` holds one per neighborhood with `tone` 0; `src/scenes/city.ts` overrides `tone` per block.
Code: `src/kit/types.ts` (`Style`, `StyleId`), `src/kit/styles.ts` (`STYLES`), `src/scenes/city.ts`.

### Wall
(Pared.) A facade that faces the camera, published by `addBuilding` for each wall of the solid (`visibleWalls`) whose normal has x > 0 or y > 0: `pts`, `normal`, `floorH` (the wall's height divided by its facade's floors: the height of one floor) and `building` (index of the solid in `Piece.solids`). The civic neighborhood's bathed facades read them.
Code: `src/kit/types.ts` (`Wall`), `src/kit/piece.ts` (`addBuilding`), `src/iso/solids.ts` (`visibleWalls`), `src/city/lights.ts`.

### Window
(Ventana.) A lit-window candidate on a wall, from `facadeWindows`: `pts`, `floor`, `wall` (the wall normal) and `building`. `src/city/lights.ts` picks which are lit.
Code: `src/kit/types.ts` (`Window`), `src/iso/facade.ts` (`facadeWindows`), `src/kit/piece.ts` (`addBuilding`), `src/city/lights.ts`.

### Slot
A place for a neon or a sign on a wall (the specs say "slot"; there is no Spanish name): `pts`, `wall` (normal), `side`, `kind` (`neon` or `sign`) and `shop` (index of the ground-floor shop). `shopRow` (`lowrise`) publishes `neon` slots per shop (plus its corner shops' side faces) when the lot has open sides, and `gallery`, `courtyardO` and `patioResidential` publish `neon` runs (`neonRun`) on their open south and east faces; `southSign` builds the south-face `sign` slot of towers at least `SIGN_MIN_H` (12) high. `src/city/lights.ts` lights the bars' neons; `src/scenes/city.ts` lights the district's signs.
Code: `src/kit/types.ts` (`Slot`), `src/kit/piece.ts` (`southSign`, `SIGN_MIN_H`), `src/kit/forms/lowrise.ts` (`neonRun`, `CAMERA_SIDES`), `src/kit/forms/courtyard.ts`, `src/kit/forms/curved.ts`, `src/city/lights.ts`, `src/scenes/city.ts`.

### Lettering
(Rotulado.) Pixi-free stroke font on a 7-unit grid (cap 7, x-height 5, descender 2, gap 2, space 3) and the generators that turn text into geometry. A `TextPlane` (`origin`, `u`, `v`) hosts the text: `diagonalWall` and `facade` for walls, `roofDiagonal` for roofs, stretched so the text reads like `diagonalWall`'s on screen. `strokeText` gives world quads on a plane, `fillText` gives text-space quads (segments plus a square on each interior vertex) for a Graphics fill, `measureText` the extent, and `layoutText` each glyph placed (left, advance, quads, fill, ink box and counters). `KERN` is the kerning in grid units for the pairs that leave too much air, and `GLYPHS` the 14 letters of Portfolio, Resume and Blog; any other character throws, except the space. No sign uses it any more: `RESUME` is marquee lettering in Neon Light, `PORTFOLIO` block lettering in CNRGN Builder and `WRITING` (the Blog zone's sign; the zone shows as Writing, its id and route stay `blog`) distressed capitals in Chainsaw Carnage painted flat on a board floating on the water, each committed only as the composed word (`src/kit/marquee-resume.ts`, `src/kit/builder-portfolio.ts`, `src/kit/chainsaw-writing.ts`, laid out by `src/kit/marquee.ts`), never as the font. The font is measured on its own: `letterGaps` gives the air between neighbouring letters, `rhythm` the ratio of the largest to the smallest of those gaps and `counterClearance` the room inside each closed counter, with counters measured at probe points placed in each glyph (`GLYPHS[c].counters`). The rules those measures are held to live in Legibility.
Code: `src/kit/lettering.ts` (`TextPlane`, `diagonalWall`, `roofDiagonal`, `facade`, `strokeText`, `fillText`, `measureText`, `layoutText`, `letterGaps`, `rhythm`, `counterClearance`, `advances`, `strokeRatio`, `overlapping`, `KERN`, `GLYPHS`).

### Sign
(Cartel.) A world object that writes a zone's name with Lettering: the Resume, Portfolio and Blog signs, one module each in `src/scenes/sign-*.ts`. The Blog sign is in the world, built by `platformAnimator`'s platform (not by `sea()`), and the Resume crown too, built by `city()` (#391); the Portfolio sign is in the world too, built by `shipyard()`, and `assembleWorld` adds its animator, `tinkerAnimator`. Each has a `piece` exhibit in the storybook, `cartel-torre`, `cartel-taller` and `plataforma`, and a `context` exhibit that crops it from the world and audits it in its scene: `cartel-torre-escena`, `cartel-taller-escena` and, one per Writing site, `plataforma-escena` and `plataforma-escena-angosta`. The Resume sign is the crown (`towerCrown`) built at `CROWN_SITE` (129, 227, 30), the center of the real tower's rooftop, where it replaced the antenna and its light, audited through `RESUME_SIGN`; `cartel-torre-escena` is a crop of the world, with no `build`. The Portfolio sign is the tinker sign built at `TINKER_SITE` (60, -24, 13.5) on the ridge of the south assembly hall, audited through `TINKER_SIGN` and animated by `tinkerAnimator` (the `l` tube and one marquee bulb exist only in its layer `tinker.lights`, from `tinkerLights`, so a static build shows the `l` panel without its letter); `cartel-taller-escena` is a crop of the world, with no `build` or `animate`. The Blog sign is the Writing platform, which has two sites (`WritingSite`, `PLATFORM_SITES`): `wide` (570, 105, 0) and `tall` (440, 400, 0). `seaPlatformAt(out, accents, site)` builds it at either; `seaPlatform` is the same piece at the origin. `BLOG_SIGNS` holds one sign per site and the lab audits each. The platform is the board: one `poly` of `officeDark` from the water (z −1) up to `BOARD_TOP` (−0.4), whose plan is the word's ink box plus `BOARD_PAD` (1.6) on every side, on a `roofDiagonal` plane; the cyan letters are painted flat on its top face at `PAINT_LIFT` (0.02) above it, and the legibility oracle's `backing` is the board. `sea()` no longer builds the platform: `platformAnimator` (`PlatformAnimator`, in `src/scenes/platform-animator.ts`) does, in two layers, `platform.solid` and `platform.lights`, and `setSite` moves it to the other site. The lights layer declares no zone: each light is veiled by its position, as Blog, at both sites. `plataforma-escena` (wide) and `plataforma-escena-angosta` (tall) are crops of the world, with no `build`; each adds its site's platform (`ContextExhibit.writingSite`) and drops the platform animator. The site is chosen by `writingSite(w, h)` (ADR 0004): `wide` when Writing's word box, at `WRITING_SITE_MARGIN` (8 px) from every edge, fits inside a `w`×`h` box under `coverView(w, h)`, else `tall`. `main.ts` computes it from `#app` (the page grid, which keeps its size while a zone opens; only `#canvas-host` changes), at boot and in the ResizeObserver, so the platform moves only with the viewport or orientation. `zoneFrame(frame, site)` extends Blog to y 440 at the tall site and `zoneView("blog", w, h, site)` shifts the camera the minimum to keep the active Writing sign inside the host (#428). `src/lab/signs.test.ts` puts every context exhibit under the sign gate (levels 1 and 2 of Legibility), and `npm run legibility` adds level 3 on the rendered pixels. On the site the signs are the navigation (ADR 0003): `zoneSigns(site)` maps each zone to its sign, the Blog one at the given Writing site, `zoneAtScreen(v, px, py, site)` hit-tests their polygons before the geography, and `labelLayout` keeps each link invisible over its sign, or visible at the edge when less than half of the word's box (`signBox`) is inside the host on either axis.

### Legibility
(Legibilidad.) The guardrails that keep a sign readable, checked at three levels. At level 1 `checkFont(text, size, width)` returns the `FONT_RULES` a word breaks (stroke and x-height ratios, air between letters, rhythm, counter clearance, advance ratios) for the font at `SIGN_FONT` (size 10.3, tube width 1.35), in vitest. At level 2 a `SignSpec`, built by `signSpec` (per-glyph plane, layout, quads and counter probes, plus the solids that back the sign), is audited by `checkSign(spec, scale, solids)` against `SIGN_RULES` as the sign projects on screen in its scene: x-height, stroke and air in pixels, WCAG contrast, baseline and vertical tilt, aspect, backing margin and occlusion, at the cover scales `COVER_LAPTOP` (1.17) and `COVER_PHONE` (0.83), also in vitest. At level 3, `npm run legibility`, needs a browser: it opens every context exhibit in agent-browser at both scales, measures contrast, halo and OCR on the rendered pixels and writes `REPORT.md` and the crops. It does not run in CI, and `REPORT.md` is only ever written by it.
Code: `src/kit/legibility.ts` (`SIGN_FONT`, `FONT_RULES`, `checkFont`, `SignSpec`, `signSpec`, `checkSign`, `SIGN_RULES`, `COVER_LAPTOP`, `COVER_PHONE`), `scripts/legibility.ts` (`main`, `startVite`), `src/lab/signs.test.ts`.
